# Promo material — Retirement Plan Case Study: From a Rough Estimate to a Reviewed Plan

## Facebook Post Snippets


**Snippet 1:** "$605,000, I want $4,500 a month, Social Security starts in five years." At a glance that sounds workable. But it hides every question that actually decides whether it works: taxes, healthcare before Medicare, inflation, which account funds each phase. A rough estimate is a starting point, not a plan.

**Snippet 2:** Here is what surprises people about improving a retirement plan: the better version often does not produce more income. It produces a cleaner structure. Better account order, smarter use of low tax brackets, less future RMD pressure. Sometimes the best improvement is not a bigger number.

**Snippet 3:** Before Medicare, the account you draw from changes your healthcare cost, not just your tax bill. In this case study, funding the 62-to-65 bridge from a careful mix of cash, brokerage, and limited pre-tax withdrawals protects ACA support while the rest of the plan stays on track.

## Newsletter Summary


A retirement plan is easiest to understand when you watch one worked from start to finish. This capstone article follows a single 62-year-old with about $605,000 across a traditional IRA, a Roth, cash, and a brokerage account, who wants roughly $4,500 a month through age 90, claims Social Security at 67, and reaches Medicare at 65. It starts with the rough estimate ($605,000, $54,000 a year, bridge five years) and shows why that hides the real questions. It then divides retirement into five phases, each with a job: the ACA bridge before Medicare, the Medicare-but-pre-Social-Security window, the years after Social Security begins, the RMD years, and later retirement. For each phase it works through the healthcare, tax, account-order, and real-income questions, then runs a Plan Health check that turns up amber areas (later real income, RMD pressure, ACA sensitivity). From there it asks sharper questions one at a time, tests improvements, and builds an improved version whose main benefit is a cleaner structure rather than more income, plus a conservative stress test to measure margin. It closes by mapping the whole loop onto the planner's Overview, Confidence, Drawdown, and Saved plans tabs, and points to a companion list of questions to ask your own plan.
