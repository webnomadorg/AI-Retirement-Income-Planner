## Facebook Post Snippets


**Snippet 1:** When to claim Social Security is one of the largest financial decisions you will make, and one of the least obvious. Claim at 62 and the money starts now but is permanently reduced. Wait to 70 and the check is about 77% larger. Here is how to model it for your own numbers.

**Snippet 2:** A surprising result: a bigger Social Security check does not always mean more monthly income. Waiting to 70 means your portfolio funds the bridge years alone, and draining it early can cost more growth than the larger check is worth. Only a plan that models claiming and withdrawals together shows it.

**Snippet 3:** For couples, the higher earner's claiming decision is really about the survivor. When one spouse dies, the survivor keeps the larger of the two benefits. Delaying can buy your spouse a bigger, inflation-adjusted safety net for life.

## Newsletter Summary


When to claim Social Security is one of the largest and least obvious retirement decisions, and this guide shows how to model it. It covers the structural numbers (about a 30% cut at 62, roughly 77% more at 70, break-even in the late 70s to around 80), how to set the claiming age in the planner and enter the right benefit figure, and how the SS Optimizer calculates the lifetime trade-off across every age from 62 to 70. Its most valuable point is that claiming age cannot be separated from your withdrawal strategy: waiting means larger early portfolio withdrawals, so a bigger check does not always mean higher monthly income, a result only visible when both are modeled together, balanced against the real longevity and survivor benefits of waiting. It also covers mid-phase claiming and split phases, and why the higher earner in a couple often waits to protect the survivor.
