# Promo material — How Taxes Change the Shape of Retirement Income

## Facebook Post Snippets


**Snippet 1:** Two retirees, same gross income, very different spendable income. The reason is tax: a dollar from a traditional 401(k), a Roth, cash, or a brokerage account is taxed in completely different ways. In retirement, where the money comes from matters as much as how much.

**Snippet 2:** The years between leaving work and starting Social Security or RMDs are often your lowest-tax years. Do nothing and a big traditional balance keeps growing into a bigger future tax bill. Those low-income years are a planning opportunity, not just a break.

**Snippet 3:** A withdrawal is never just a withdrawal. It can raise your bracket, tax more of your Social Security, and lift your healthcare costs at the same time. That is why the real cost of a withdrawal is often higher than the tax rate alone suggests.

## Newsletter Summary


Taxes change the shape of retirement income more than most people expect. Two retirees with the same gross income can keep very different amounts, and two plans that withdraw the same total over a lifetime can pay very different tax, purely on timing and source. This article makes the case for treating tax as part of the income plan. It separates cash flow from taxable income, shows how account type (traditional, Roth, cash, brokerage) sets the result, and explains why the low-income years before Social Security and RMDs are a lever rather than just a lull. It covers bracket space and the difference between marginal and effective rates, the way ordinary withdrawals can push more of your Social Security into tax, why RMDs from 73 can force income you did not ask for, and how to think about Roth conversions as how-much-which-years-at-what-cost rather than good-or-bad. Finally it shows how the planner surfaces estimated tax and net income on every phase card, models per-phase Roth conversions and a configurable RMD age, runs a Tax Bracket Efficiency check in the Plan Health Score, and documents every calculation on the Tax & ACA tab.
