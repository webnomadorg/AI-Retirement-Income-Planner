**Snippet 2:** Retiring at 56? ## Newsletter Summary


Most retirement calculators assume a standard path, but real lives vary, and a plan has to bend to fit. This article walks through three worked case studies in the AI Retirement Income Planner. The early retiree before 59.5, where the planner adds a bridge phase and prices how the 401(k) is reached — left untouched, drawn with the 10% penalty, or under the Rule of 55. The US expat in Southeast Asia, where a non-US currency triggers foreign-residence mode that excludes US ACA and Medicare costs, displays the plan in local currency, and often shows low or zero federal tax while US filing still applies. And the digital nomad on freelance income, whose real challenge is the inflation gap between a stable-feeling target today and the much larger nominal withdrawals needed decades later. The common thread is that one month-by-month engine models all of them, and the right measure of success is net income you can actually spend, not the lowest tax figure. It also notes the planner models foreign-residence assumptions rather than full local tax law, so cross-border specifics need a professional.
