# Promo material — What to Do If Your Retirement Balances Are Lower Than Expected

## Schema Notes


Use `Article` schema with `FAQPage` schema for the FAQ section. Suggested FAQ questions:

- What should I do first if my retirement balances are lower than expected?
- Does a lower balance mean I need to cut spending right away?
- Should I change investments after balances fall?
- Can RMDs still matter if my balances are lower?
- Can the planner help compare recovery options?

## Facebook Post Snippets


**Snippet 1:**  
Lower retirement balances do not always mean the same thing. First find the cause: markets, spending, taxes, healthcare, one-time costs, or an old assumption.

**Snippet 2:**  
Before cutting retirement spending across the board, compare the options: smaller withdrawals, part-time income, delayed expenses, withdrawal changes, or a housing scenario.

**Snippet 3:**  
The most useful response to lower retirement balances is a fresh plan from today, with current balances, current spending, and realistic fallback scenarios.

## Newsletter Summary


This article explains what retirees can do when retirement balances are lower than expected. It covers confirming the shortfall, identifying causes, measuring withdrawal pressure, reviewing income timing, taxes, healthcare, RMDs, and building recovery scenarios for spending, income, withdrawals, housing, and stress cases.
