# Promo material — Your First Retirement Planning Session: Build a Baseline Step by Step

## Facebook Post Snippets


**Snippet 1:** The goal of your first retirement planning session is not a perfect plan. It is a clear baseline you can review. A baseline reveals problems: if it shows weak income, a low ending balance, or a healthcare warning, that is the session working. Now you know what to review.

**Snippet 2:** The most common first-session mistake is trying to make every number perfect before you start. Use reasonable estimates. A rough baseline you can question beats a precise plan you never build. You can always refine the inputs later, once the structure is in place.

**Snippet 3:** One habit makes a first planning session work: save the baseline before you change anything. Then test one change at a time and keep an improved version and a conservative stress test as separate scenarios. That way you always know what actually helped.

## Newsletter Summary


The goal of a first retirement planning session is not a perfect plan but a clear baseline: a first working version, built from current balances, estimated income and spending, and reasonable assumptions, organized well enough to review. This article turns the whole framework into a practical workflow. Part one gathers the inputs (basic shape, account balances by type, growth assumptions, income sources, essential and discretionary spending, healthcare by phase, and tax parameters). Part two builds a baseline withdrawal pattern, reads the plan phase by phase on the Overview before changing anything, and saves the baseline under a clear name. Part three reviews and improves: check Plan Health, find and focus on the weakest phase, then change one thing at a time while testing Social Security timing, tax bracket position, healthcare-sensitive years, and withdrawal strategies, using the optional AI review to summarize risks and propose changes you approve. Part four saves an improved scenario and a conservative stress test as separate versions and writes down the decisions. It closes by showing how the planner makes this concrete, opening pre-filled with an example, offering a setup wizard, and mapping each step onto the Overview, Confidence, Saved plans, Drawdown, and AI tabs.
