## Facebook Post Snippets


- Monte Carlo and historical backtesting are different retirement planning tools. One creates many possible future paths. The other tests the plan against real past market periods.

- A confidence score can help, but it is not the plan. Look at weak years, taxes, healthcare costs, Social Security timing, and survivor outcomes too.

- Historical backtesting gives real-world context. Monte Carlo gives a wider range of possibilities. Stress tests answer the direct what-if questions.

- A retirement plan should not rely on one risk score. Use Monte Carlo, historical backtesting, stress tests, and scenario comparison together.

## Newsletter Summary


Monte Carlo simulation and historical backtesting answer different retirement planning questions. Monte Carlo creates many randomized future paths and can show a range of possible outcomes. Historical backtesting runs the plan through real past market periods. Both can help, and both can mislead when used alone. This article explains how to use both alongside stress tests, taxes, healthcare costs, Social Security timing, RMDs, drawdown strategies, Plan Health, Confidence scoring, and scenario comparison.
