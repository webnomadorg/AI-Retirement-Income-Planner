# Promo material — Keeping a Retirement Plan Current: Annual Review and Replan From Today

## Facebook Post Snippets


**Snippet 1:** A retirement plan is not a document you finish and file away. It is a working model built on today's balances, tax rules, and assumptions, and those change. A plan stays useful only when it is updated to match, which is why an annual review beats a detailed plan left to drift for years.

**Snippet 2:** Replanning from today re-anchors an old projection to where you actually are: your current age and real balances. If you are already partway through retirement, the past phases have happened. A fresh plan from where you stand now usually beats a precise projection from where you used to be.

**Snippet 3:** Save versions of your retirement plan. A baseline plus a revised version lets you see exactly what changed and why, trace which assumption moved the plan, and roll back if the new version looks worse. Version control turns a plan into a process instead of a file you overwrite and forget.

## Newsletter Summary


A retirement plan is a working model, not a one-time document, and this article is about keeping it current. It explains why plans drift (investment performance, tax and healthcare changes, COLA, currency, and life events), then lays out a practical annual-review habit that is simple enough to actually do. It covers what to update first (balances by account type, spending split into essential and discretionary, and the tax, healthcare, return, and inflation assumptions), why saving a baseline and comparing versions matters, and how replanning from today re-anchors an old projection to your current age and actual balances. It notes the life events that should trigger a review right away, and why Plan Health should be re-run after any update since a new tax bracket, FPL value, or IRMAA threshold can change the picture in ways that are easy to miss. Finally it shows how the planner supports all of this: a Replan control, three saved-plan version slots with side-by-side comparison, a plan-status chip that signs off a plan and sets a 12-month review reminder while tracking drift, a Fetch-current-tax-rates button, change-since-baseline markers, and a model that recalculates the moment anything changes.
