# Promo material — Comparing Retirement Withdrawal Strategies: 4%, VPW, Buckets

## Facebook Post Snippets


**Snippet 1:** The 4% rule is a useful benchmark, not a plan. It knows nothing about your taxes, when Social Security starts, or whether you need ACA coverage before Medicare. Use it to sanity-check your withdrawals, then build a real strategy around your actual income sources.

**Snippet 2:** Flexible withdrawal strategies like VPW protect your portfolio by letting income fall in bad years. That is fine for travel and extras, but stressful if the money covers essentials. The fix is often floor-and-upside: reliable income for needs, flexible withdrawals for wants.

**Snippet 3:** When comparing withdrawal strategies, do not just pick the one that pays the most. Higher income usually means higher risk. Read across real income, ending balance, portfolio survival, and how it feels in a bad market. The best strategy is the one that fits your plan, not the biggest number.

## Newsletter Summary


Every retirement plan needs a withdrawal strategy, and this article compares the main ones and, more importantly, shows how to choose between them. It explains why an intentional rule beats improvising, then walks through the 4% rule (a benchmark, not a plan), variable percentage withdrawal (adaptable but variable), guardrails (a middle path that adjusts only at set boundaries), the bucket strategy (organizing money by time horizon), and floor-and-upside (separating essential income from discretionary spending). It covers matching a strategy to your temperament, income sources, and tax and healthcare situation, and stresses that a strategy has to be usable, not just mathematically sound. It explains how to read a comparison by fit rather than by the highest headline income, looking across real income, ending balance, portfolio survival, variability, and stress-test results. Finally it shows how the planner's Drawdown tab compares the 4% rule, variable percentage, buckets, floor and upside, guardrails, and Monte Carlo against your own plan, each with plain-language insights and an Ask AI button.
