# Promo material — Retirement Income Is a Timeline: How to Plan in Phases

## Facebook Post Snippets


**Snippet 1:** "I need $4,000 a month." Useful to know, but it doesn't tell you how to produce that income safely, and a $4,000 target before Medicare is a different plan than $4,000 after. Retirement income is a timeline, not one number. Here's how to plan it in phases.

**Snippet 2:** A $10,000 withdrawal can fill an unused tax bracket in one phase and trigger an IRMAA surcharge in the next. The same dollar amount, a completely different result. That's why retirement planning should follow the timeline, not one fixed withdrawal number.

**Snippet 3:** Break retirement into phases (before Social Security, before and after Medicare, the pre-RMD window, the RMD years) and each period gets a clear job. Five smaller questions are far easier to answer than one giant retirement calculation.

## Newsletter Summary


Retirement is easier to plan when you treat it as a timeline instead of one long, uniform period. Age milestones (59½, 62, 65, 67, 70, 73) mark the points where the plan changes: penalty-free account access, Social Security, Medicare, full retirement age, and required minimum distributions. This article shows how to break retirement into phases, each with its own income sources, healthcare rules, tax situation, and a job to do, and how the ending balance of one phase becomes the starting balance of the next. It also shows how the planner's Overview tab builds that timeline as editable phase cards that recalculate whenever you change a withdrawal, a claiming age, or an assumption.
