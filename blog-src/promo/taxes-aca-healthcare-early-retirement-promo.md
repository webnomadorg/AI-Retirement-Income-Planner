## Facebook Post Snippets


**Snippet 1:** In the years before Medicare, the biggest lever on your costs is not how much you spend. It is how much taxable income you show, and which accounts it comes from. Cross one line and health insurance can jump by thousands.

**Snippet 2:** One number, your MAGI, controls your ACA subsidies before 65 and your Medicare surcharges after. Cash is invisible to it, 401(k) withdrawals count in full, and Roth withdrawals do not count at all. That difference is a planning tool.

**Snippet 3:** The ACA subsidy cliff returned in 2026. Above roughly 400% of the poverty level the subsidy can disappear entirely. Staying under about 250% with a Silver plan unlocks extra cost-sharing help. Where your income lands is a choice.

## Newsletter Summary


In the years before Medicare, the largest lever on retirement cost is how much taxable income you show and which accounts it comes from. This guide explains the mechanics: the three tax buckets (cash, tax-deferred, and taxable brokerage) and how each affects MAGI, why MAGI is the number that controls ACA subsidies before 65 and Medicare IRMAA surcharges after, the ACA subsidy cliff (back in effect for 2026 after the enhanced subsidies expired) and the cost-sharing sweet spot below 250% of the poverty level, how IRMAA works on a two-year lookback, and why the planner inflates brackets and thresholds forward automatically. It also covers nominal versus real income, how Social Security is taxed through provisional income, optional state tax, and how living abroad changes the picture, then ties it together into a phase-by-phase strategy. The planner flags every threshold as you plan.
