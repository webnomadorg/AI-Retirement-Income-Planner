# Promo material — How to Prepare for Retirement: Building an Income Plan for the Next 30 Years

## Facebook Post Snippets


**Snippet 1:** You spent 30 or 40 years learning how to earn an income. Retirement does not remove that responsibility, it changes where the paycheck comes from. Preparing for retirement means designing the income that replaces the salary, then testing whether it holds.

**Snippet 2:** A savings number is not a retirement plan. The plan is the part that decides when Social Security starts, which accounts fund which years, what taxes and healthcare will take, and what happens if markets fall early.

**Snippet 3:** The most useful question in retirement planning is rarely "how much do I have?" It is "how much of this can I actually spend, in which years, and what happens if I am wrong?"

## Newsletter Summary


You spent decades learning how to earn an income. Preparing for retirement is the work of learning how to turn what you accumulated into one. This article lays out what a retirement income plan has to cover: retirement as a sequence of connected phases rather than one averaged year, the paycheck you now build yourself from Social Security, pensions, withdrawals, and other income, the trade-offs behind retiring earlier or delaying benefits, the difference between gross income and what you can actually spend after taxes and healthcare, why one optimistic projection is not a test, and why the survivor scenario deserves attention before it is needed. It also covers how the AI Retirement Income Planner models each of those, how optional AI assistance sits beside the calculation engine rather than replacing it, and why a plan you own, keep private, and can revisit for years beats a one-off report.
