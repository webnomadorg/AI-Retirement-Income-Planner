# Promo material — Inflation and Real Retirement Income: Nominal vs Real, COLA, and the Floor

## Facebook Post Snippets


**Snippet 1:** A retirement plan can show income rising from $5,000 a month at 62 to $5,500 at 80 and look like it is improving. In real terms, after inflation, the later figure may buy less. The dollar amount went up. The spending power went down. That gap is what real income reveals.

**Snippet 2:** Inflation compounds. At about 3% a year, prices roughly double over 24 years. A lifestyle that costs $4,000 a month today could cost far more late in a 30-year retirement. The longer the plan, the more inflation matters, and the more valuable a COLA-adjusted income floor becomes.

**Snippet 3:** A level pension of $2,000 a month feels comfortable early and tight later, because fixed income loses purchasing power every year. Social Security is different: it adjusts with a COLA. How much of your income floor is inflation-protected is one of the underrated questions that decides a long retirement.

## Newsletter Summary


Inflation reshapes what retirement income can buy, slowly enough to be easy to miss and steadily enough to matter over a long plan. This article makes the case for reading a plan in real income, the value of future dollars in today's purchasing power, rather than nominal dollars alone. It explains why the two answer different questions, how inflation compounds (about a doubling of prices over 24 years at 3%), and why not every expense rises equally, with healthcare often climbing faster. It covers how fixed income like a level pension loses ground while a COLA-adjusted benefit such as Social Security or a Triple-Locked UK State Pension holds up, why the income floor should be reviewed in real terms, and how lifestyle spending is rarely level (the retirement spending smile). It looks at real returns, the split role of cash, and the currency layer expats face. Finally it shows how the planner puts real income on every phase card in today's money, lets you set general, healthcare, and COLA inflation rates, charts real net income by phase, stress-tests higher inflation, and flags when real income slips below your floor.
