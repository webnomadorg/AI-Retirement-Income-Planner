# Promo material — 50+ Questions to Ask Your Retirement Plan (A Review Checklist)

## Facebook Post Snippets


**Snippet 1:** The quality of a retirement plan review depends on the quality of your questions. "Am I okay?" gets a vague answer. "Would a modest Roth conversion at 66 push me into IRMAA?" gets an answer you can act on. Here are fifty-plus specific questions to ask your own plan.

**Snippet 2:** You do not need to answer every question about your retirement plan. You need to ask the right handful at your weakest phase. Most plans improve fastest when you aim your best questions at the phase under the most pressure, not when you spread small tweaks everywhere.

**Snippet 3:** A question worth asking your retirement plan is usually worth testing as a scenario. If you cannot clearly answer "does real income still cover essentials at 85?" then that is not a worry to sit with, it is a version to build and compare. That is how a plan becomes something you can improve.

## Newsletter Summary


A good retirement plan is one you can question, and the quality of a review depends on the quality of the questions. Broad questions produce vague answers; specific questions, ones that name a phase, an account, a threshold, or a single assumption, produce answers you can act on. This companion resource is a structured checklist of more than fifty such questions, grouped by theme: timeline, income, income floor, real income and inflation, ending balances, taxes, healthcare before Medicare, Medicare and IRMAA, account order, Social Security timing, Roth conversions, withdrawal strategy, stress tests, AI Chat prompts, and annual review. It explains how to use the list (work by theme, start at your weakest phase, and treat any question without a clear answer as a scenario to test), what makes a good question (specific, and about a trade-off rather than a verdict), and how the planner gives each theme somewhere to land across the Overview, Confidence, Drawdown, Scenarios, Stress test, Saved plans, and optional AI Chat tabs. It pairs with the worked case study, which shows these questions being asked one at a time about a single plan.
