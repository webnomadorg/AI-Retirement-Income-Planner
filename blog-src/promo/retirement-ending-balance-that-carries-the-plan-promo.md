# Promo material — Retirement Ending Balance: The Number That Carries the Plan

## Facebook Post Snippets


**Snippet 1:** A retirement plan has two jobs: produce income now, and leave enough for the next phase. Most plans nail the first and ignore the second, which is how a plan looks great today and fragile later. The number that links them is the ending balance.

**Snippet 2:** A phase can hit its $5,000-a-month income target and still be a problem, if it drained the 401(k) too fast to get there. In retirement, "did I hit my income number" is only half the question. The other half is "what was left afterward?"

**Snippet 3:** Two retirees both end a phase with $500,000. One is almost all pre-tax (big future RMDs and taxes); the other is split with Roth and cash (far more flexible). The ending balance is not just how much remains. It is where it remains.

## Newsletter Summary


A retirement plan has two jobs: create income for the phase you are in, and leave enough for the phase that follows. The number that connects them is the ending balance, and this article makes the case for reading it phase by phase. Retirement is a chain of balances, where each phase's ending balance is the next phase's starting balance, so an early decision ripples through everything after it. A phase can meet its income target and still weaken the future by drawing down too fast, and the difference between a sound plan and a fragile one is usually planned drawdown versus accidental depletion, which comes down to visibility. It covers sequence-of-returns risk, why the account mix of the balance (pre-tax, Roth, cash, brokerage) matters as much as the total, and how taxes and healthcare reshape future balances. Finally it shows how the planner surfaces the ending balance on every phase card, charts it by account and across three return scenarios on the Balance tab, stress-tests it, and recognizes a deliberate drawdown when guaranteed income takes over.
