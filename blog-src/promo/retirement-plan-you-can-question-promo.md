# Promo material — Build a Retirement Plan You Can Question (Not Just One Number)

## Facebook Post Snippets


**Snippet 1:** "I have $600,000." "This calculator says 85%." Neither answers the questions that actually decide whether retirement works. Here's why your plan should be something you can question, not one number.

**Snippet 2:** A single "chance of success" score can look healthy while one phase of your plan is quietly fragile. A working model shows you where the risk is, and lets you test a fix.

**Snippet 3:** Don't wait for perfect inputs. Build a baseline from reasonable estimates, save it, then test the alternatives you're unsure about. A plan doesn't need to be perfect to be useful.

## Newsletter Summary


A savings total or a single "probability of success" score can't answer the real retirement questions: how much income survives taxes and healthcare, what happens before Medicare and Social Security, and whether today's withdrawal weakens a later phase. This opening article of our framework series makes the case for a retirement plan you can question: a working model, organized as a timeline, that shows net and real income, taxes, healthcare, and ending balances so you can change one thing and see the effect. It also covers the most useful first step, building a baseline instead of waiting for perfect inputs.
