# Promo material — How to Check a Retirement Plan From Several Angles

## Facebook Post Snippets


**Snippet 1:** A retirement plan can look strong from one angle and weak from another. It may produce plenty of income while leaning too hard on withdrawals, or keep this year's tax low while building a future RMD problem. No single number tells the whole story, which is why a plan should be checked from several angles at once.

**Snippet 2:** You do not need every retirement check to be green. Some amber flags are deliberate: spending more early while you are healthy, or crossing an IRMAA line for a worthwhile Roth conversion. The real question is never "is the score perfect" but "do I understand the trade-off?"

**Snippet 3:** When improving a retirement plan, change one thing at a time. If you adjust retirement age, Social Security timing, withdrawals, returns, and inflation all at once, you will never know which change helped. Baseline, then one change, then review. That is how a plan becomes understandable.

## Newsletter Summary


A retirement plan can look strong from one angle and weak from another, which is why the strongest review checks it from several angles at once and treats it like a system rather than a scoreboard. This article explains why no single number, not a savings target, a withdrawal rate, an income figure, a final balance, or a probability score, can judge a plan on its own. It walks through the angles worth checking (income adequacy, real income, ending balances, tax efficiency, ACA and IRMAA sensitivity, RMD pressure, withdrawal sustainability, stress resilience, and account mix), the green-amber-red model, and why not every check needs to be green when an amber item is a deliberate choice. It covers changing one assumption at a time, using baseline, improved, and conservative scenarios to separate preference from risk, and the danger of chasing a score. Finally it shows how the planner builds this into a Confidence tab with up to 12 tiered checks across four categories, a 0 to 100 score and an independent completeness verdict, a per-check Ask AI button, and Monte Carlo and stress-test confirmation, and it links to the full Plan Health how-to.
