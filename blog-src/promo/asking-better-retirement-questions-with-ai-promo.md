# Promo material — Asking Better Retirement Questions and Reviewing Your Plan With AI

## Facebook Post Snippets


**Snippet 1:** Most people start retirement planning with the biggest question: can I retire? It is understandable, but too broad to act on. A better question points at something specific: which phase has the weakest ending balance, or do I have unused tax bracket space before RMDs begin? The clearer the question, the more useful the answer.

**Snippet 2:** The best retirement questions ask about trade-offs, not just outcomes. Not "should I withdraw more" but "if I increase withdrawals in this phase, how does it affect tax, healthcare, and the next phase's ending balance?" A trade-off question gives you a decision you can actually weigh.

**Snippet 3:** An AI retirement assistant should propose, not decide. In a good tool, when you ask it to change your plan, it returns proposed edits you review and approve, checked against the real simulation first. The planner calculates, the AI helps explain and propose, and you decide.

## Newsletter Summary


A retirement plan becomes far more useful once you know what to ask it, and this article is about asking better questions and then reviewing the plan with optional AI. It explains why specific questions beat broad ones, how to attach a question to a phase, how to hunt for the plan's weakest link, and why the most useful questions ask about trade-offs (what a change improves and what it weakens) rather than outcomes alone. It then covers reviewing with AI: how the planner's assistant is optional and plan-aware, working from your actual ages, phases, balances, taxes, and health checks rather than a blank box, and why that context makes the quality of the question matter even more. It stresses that the AI reviews while you decide, that every suggestion should be checked against real numbers and current rules, and shows how the planner keeps control in your hands: multi-provider bring-your-own-key, a Plan-with-AI sidebar, Plan versus Learn modes, one-click starter prompts, an Ask-AI-about-this-comparison button, and proposed changes you review and approve before anything is applied.
