## Facebook Post Snippets


- Retirement planning data is personal. Before choosing software, ask where your plan is stored, whether an account is required, and whether bank links or AI features are optional.

- A private retirement planner still needs real planning power. Privacy helps, but the tool should also model taxes, healthcare, Social Security, withdrawals, and risk.

- Local files and cloud accounts are different privacy models. Neither is automatically right for everyone. The key is choosing the workflow you understand and trust.

- If you do not want a bank-connected app, look for a retirement planner that supports manual entry, local storage, export/import, and clear privacy controls.

## Newsletter Summary


This article explains how to evaluate retirement planning software through a privacy lens. It compares local browser planners, local spreadsheets, cloud planners, bank-connected apps, and advisor portals. It also explains why privacy has to be paired with planning depth: taxes, healthcare, Social Security, Roth conversions, RMDs, scenarios, Monte Carlo, historical backtesting, Plan Health, and Confidence scoring. The AI Retirement Income Planner is positioned as a private browser-based planner for users who want no account requirement, no bank connection, local storage, manual export/import, and detailed retirement income modeling.
