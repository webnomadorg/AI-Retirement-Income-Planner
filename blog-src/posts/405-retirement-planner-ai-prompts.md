## The Six Prompts, One by One

### Summarize & flag risks

This is your start-every-session prompt. It gives a 60-second sanity check: the AI summarizes what your plan is doing and flags the top three or four things worth worrying about. You will often discover risks you did not know were there. Begin here, then dig into specifics with the others.

### Tax bracket check

Answers one question with precision: in every phase, am I staying inside the 12% bracket, and if not, by how much? The AI reports your headroom phase by phase, which is exactly the information you need to decide whether to do a Roth conversion or take an extra 401(k) withdrawal while the low brackets have room.

### ACA / CSR check

For anyone retiring before 65, this is the prompt that prevents expensive mistakes. It tells you, phase by phase, whether you are eligible for premium subsidies and cost-sharing reductions, and how close you are to the 400% federal poverty level cliff. It connects directly to the mechanics in [taxes, ACA, and healthcare in early retirement](/blog/taxes-aca-healthcare-early-retirement.html).

### Withdrawal strategy

Asks the AI to look at how you draw across all phases, 401(k) versus cash versus Roth versus brokerage, and suggest improvements to tax efficiency and account-depletion timing. Most retirees get one of those right and miss the other; the AI is asked to consider both at once.

### Propose optimizations

This is the action prompt. It proposes specific numeric changes to optimize your plan, aiming to keep you in the 12% bracket and preserve ACA cost-sharing eligibility where possible. Its output is what feeds the proposed-changes panel described below.

### Roth conversion advice

Answers the question many retirees eventually reach: should I convert, in which phases, and how much? The AI sizes conversions to fill a bracket without spilling into the next one, respecting every cliff and threshold, and returns phase-specific dollar amounts you can apply. For a deeper treatment, the planner also has a dedicated Roth conversion tool.

## Applying the AI's Proposed Changes

When a prompt returns changes, they arrive as a **proposed-changes panel**: a list of exact field edits, each with a checkbox.

[IMAGE ai prompts 3]

The workflow is simple and safe:

1. **Read the proposals.** Each one names the phase and the exact change, and the panel confirms which Plan Health checks it preserves.
2. **Tick the ones you want, untick the rest.** You are never forced to take all of them.
3. **Click apply selected.** The plan recalculates immediately with only the changes you approved.

This is the heart of the design: the AI can propose, but **you approve**. Every proposal is dry-run through the planner's own calculation engine before it is applied, so the numbers you see are the planner's, not the AI's guesswork. The [user-approved proposal workflow](/blog/how-to-use-the-ai-co-pilot.html) is covered in more depth in the co-pilot guide.

## The Trade-Off the AI Will Show You

One of the most valuable things the AI does is surface a choice rather than pretend there is a single right answer. A common example: in the pre-Medicare phases you often **cannot** both fill your low tax brackets *and* keep your income low enough for the best ACA cost-sharing reductions. Withdrawing more to use cheap bracket space raises your MAGI and can cost you the subsidy.

When this comes up, the AI lays out both paths, more tax arbitrage versus more ACA benefit, and tells you it is a decision only you can make based on your expected medical costs and priorities. That honesty is the point. A tool that quietly optimized one and silently sacrificed the other would be doing you a disservice. Here, you see the fork and choose.

## Ask Your Own Questions, and the AI Keeps Up

You are not limited to the six buttons. You can type any question: "What is my effective tax rate in each phase?" or "What happens if my 401(k) grows at 4% instead of 7%?" The AI answers against your real plan.

And if you change your plan after asking something, the chat notices. It shows a note that the plan has been updated and will be included in your next reply, and once you send that message it confirms the AI is up to date with your plan. So you can ask, adjust a number, ask again, and trust that the AI is always reasoning about your current plan rather than a stale copy. This makes a natural loop: ask, adjust, re-check, improve.

## FAQ

### Do the AI prompts see my actual plan or just general information?

Each of the six prompts automatically attaches your complete plan, every phase, balance, withdrawal, and tax parameter, so the AI answers about your real numbers, not a generic example. It even knows your exact headroom to the next tax bracket, the IRMAA threshold, and the ACA cliff, and treats your passing Plan Health checks as constraints.

### Will the AI change my plan on its own?

No. The AI proposes changes as a panel of exact edits with checkboxes. You choose which to keep and click apply; nothing changes until you approve it. Every proposal is run through the planner's own calculation engine first, so the resulting numbers are the planner's, not the AI's estimate.

### Which prompt should I start with?

Start with "Summarize & flag risks" for a 60-second sanity check on the whole plan. Then use the more specific prompts, tax bracket check, ACA/CSR check, withdrawal strategy, to dig into anything it surfaced, and "Propose optimizations" or "Roth conversion advice" when you want concrete changes you can apply.

### Why does the AI refuse to just tell me the single best plan?

Because some goals genuinely trade off against each other. In early retirement you often cannot both fill low tax brackets and keep income low enough for the best ACA cost-sharing, since more withdrawals raise your MAGI. The AI shows you both paths and the trade-off rather than silently sacrificing one, because the right choice depends on your priorities. It is educational assistance, not advice.

### Can I use the AI features without paying for an API key?

Yes. The built-in AI Chat needs your own key (a few cents per conversation), but the planner also generates a copy-and-paste prompt containing your plan summary that you can paste into any free or paid AI assistant you already use. The core planner and all its calculations work with no key at all.
